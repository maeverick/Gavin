Subject: Company Law — Powers of Directors, Division of Powers
Source: Automatic Self-Cleansing Filter Syndicate Co Ltd v Cuninghame [1906] 2 Ch 34 (Court of Appeal)

CASE NAME: Automatic Self-Cleansing Filter Syndicate Co Ltd v Cuninghame [1906] 2 Ch 34

FACTS:
The company's articles of association vested the management of the company in the board of directors. The shareholders passed an ordinary resolution directing the directors to sell the company's assets. The directors refused to comply, asserting that under the articles the power to manage (including to sell assets) was vested in them, not the general meeting. The shareholders argued that as principals, they could direct their agents (the directors).

ISSUE:
Where the articles vest management powers in the directors, can a simple majority of shareholders override the directors' decision?

HELD (Court of Appeal):
No. Where the articles vest the management of the company in the directors, the shareholders cannot by ordinary resolution override the directors' exercise of those powers. The articles constitute a contract between the company and its members, and the powers vested in the directors cannot be taken back by ordinary resolution. To override the directors, shareholders would need to alter the articles (special resolution) or remove the directors.

KEY PRINCIPLES:
1. DIVISION OF POWERS: The constitution (articles) of a company divides powers between the general meeting and the board of directors
2. BOARD AUTONOMY: Where the articles vest management in the board, the board has an independent authority — it is not merely an agent of the shareholders
3. OVERRIDING THE BOARD: Shareholders may only override the board by:
   (a) Altering the articles by special resolution (75% majority)
   (b) Removing directors under Section 288 CAMA 2020 (ordinary resolution with special notice)
4. SHAREHOLDERS IN GENERAL MEETING retain residual powers where:
   (a) The board cannot act (e.g., deadlock, no quorum)
   (b) The articles reserve the matter to shareholders
   (c) The Companies Act requires shareholder approval (e.g., substantial property transactions)

CAMA 2020 — DIVISION OF POWERS:
Section 63 CAMA 2020: Directors manage the business and affairs of the company
Section 288 CAMA 2020: Shareholders may remove directors by ordinary resolution with special notice
Section 265 CAMA 2020: Substantial property transactions require shareholder approval

APPLICATION IN NIGERIA:
Automatic Self-Cleansing is applied in Nigerian company law disputes about the respective powers of directors and shareholders. Relevant in boardroom disputes, takeover situations, and cases where shareholders seek to direct management decisions. Applied under CAMA 2020 provisions on directors' duties and powers.
