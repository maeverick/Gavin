Subject: Professional Ethics — Rules of Professional Conduct for Legal Practitioners
Source: Rules of Professional Conduct for Legal Practitioners 2007; Legal Practitioners Act (Cap L11 LFN 2004); NBA v Kehinde and key ethics cases

KEY PROFESSIONAL ETHICS PRINCIPLES IN NIGERIA:

THE RULES OF PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT FOR LEGAL PRACTITIONERS 2007 (RPC):
Enacted pursuant to Section 12 of the Legal Practitioners Act by the General Council of the Bar.

RULE 1 — DUTY TO THE COURT:
A lawyer's duty to the court supersedes the duty to the client. A lawyer shall not:
- Knowingly make false statements of law or fact to the court
- Present evidence the lawyer knows to be false
- Offer inadmissible evidence knowing it is inadmissible
- Obstruct the administration of justice

RULE 14 — LAWYER-CLIENT RELATIONSHIP:
- Lawyers must maintain absolute confidentiality of client communications
- Legal professional privilege: communications between lawyer and client for the purpose of legal advice are privileged (not disclosable)
- Duty to act in the client's best interests within the law

RULE 15 — CONFLICT OF INTEREST:
A lawyer must not act where there is a conflict of interest between:
- Two current clients
- A current client and a former client
- The client and the lawyer's personal interests
A lawyer who discovers a conflict must withdraw unless all parties give informed consent.

RULE 32 — UNDERTAKINGS:
- A lawyer's undertaking given to another lawyer or to the court is binding
- Breach of an undertaking is a disciplinary offence
- Undertakings given without authority of the client are personally binding on the lawyer

RULE 16 — FEES:
- Fees must be fair and reasonable
- Written retainer agreements are encouraged
- Contingency fees (percentage of damages) are permitted in certain matters but regulated
- A lawyer cannot charge an unconscionable fee

DUTY TO DISCLOSE AND CANDOUR:
- A lawyer must be candid with the court — cannot knowingly mislead
- Must disclose adverse legal authority even if not cited by the other side
- Must correct false impressions created in court

SOLICITOR-CLIENT PRIVILEGE (Legal Professional Privilege):
RULE: Communications between a lawyer and their client made for the purpose of legal advice are absolutely privileged and cannot be disclosed without the client's consent.
EXCEPTIONS:
1. Future crime or fraud: privilege does not extend to communications facilitating future crimes
2. Client waiver: privilege belongs to the client and may be waived
3. Crime-fraud exception: where the communication itself was made to facilitate crime

KEY DISCIPLINARY OFFENCES:
1. Misappropriation of client funds
2. Deception of the court
3. Breach of undertaking
4. Acting while disqualified
5. Touting for business (except permitted advertising)
6. Sharing fees with non-lawyers
7. Conflict of interest without disclosure
8. Breach of confidentiality

TRIBUNAL FOR DISCIPLINARY PROCEEDINGS:
The Legal Practitioners Disciplinary Committee (LPDC) handles disciplinary proceedings against lawyers. Sanctions range from reprimand to striking off the roll (disbarment). Appeals lie to the Court of Appeal.

APPLICATION IN NIGERIA:
Professional ethics is a compulsory subject for the Nigerian Bar examination. These principles are applied in disciplinary proceedings, court proceedings involving legal practitioners, and professional responsibility examinations. Every practising lawyer in Nigeria is subject to the Rules of Professional Conduct.
