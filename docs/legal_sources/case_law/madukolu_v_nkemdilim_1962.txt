Subject: Civil Procedure — Jurisdiction
Source: Madukolu v Nkemdilim [1962] 1 All NLR 587 — Nigerian Supreme Court

CASE NAME: Madukolu v Nkemdilim [1962] 1 All NLR 587 (also cited as [1962] 2 SCNLR 341)

FACTS:
The case concerned whether the High Court had jurisdiction to entertain the matter brought before it. The court was required to determine the conditions precedent that must be satisfied before a court can properly exercise jurisdiction.

ISSUE:
What are the conditions that must be satisfied before a court can exercise jurisdiction?

HELD (Supreme Court, Bairamian FJ):
A court is competent to hear a matter, and a party is entitled to invoke its jurisdiction, if:
1. The court is properly constituted as regards numbers and qualifications of members
2. The subject matter of the case is within the court's jurisdiction, and there is no feature of the case which prevents the court from exercising its jurisdiction
3. The case comes before the court initiated by due process of law
4. Where applicable, any condition precedent to the exercise of jurisdiction has been fulfilled

KEY PRINCIPLES — THE MADUKOLU TEST FOR JURISDICTION:
CONDITION 1 — PROPER CONSTITUTION: The court must be duly constituted — proper number of judges, duly qualified and appointed
CONDITION 2 — SUBJECT MATTER JURISDICTION:
- The subject matter must be within the class of cases the court is authorised to hear
- Territorial jurisdiction must be satisfied
- The parties must have capacity to be before that court
CONDITION 3 — DUE PROCESS:
- Proceedings must have been initiated by the proper process (writ, summons, etc.)
- Proper service must have been effected
CONDITION 4 — CONDITIONS PRECEDENT:
- Statutory conditions must be fulfilled (e.g., pre-action notice requirements)
- Demand letters where required
- Notice to government under Public Officers Protection Act

CONSEQUENCES OF LACK OF JURISDICTION:
- Proceedings conducted without jurisdiction are a nullity
- No court can confer jurisdiction on itself
- Parties cannot confer jurisdiction by consent where the court has none
- Objection to jurisdiction can be raised at any stage, even on appeal

EXCLUSIVE JURISDICTION OF THE FEDERAL HIGH COURT:
Section 251 Constitution 1999 grants exclusive jurisdiction to the Federal High Court over:
- Federal revenue matters
- Federal government as a party
- Admiralty
- Banking and currency
- Copyright, patent, trademarks
- Citizenship and naturalisation

APPLICATION IN NIGERIA:
Madukolu v Nkemdilim is the most cited case on jurisdiction in Nigerian law. Applied in every civil and criminal case where jurisdiction is challenged. The four-condition test is applied by all levels of Nigerian courts. Understanding this case is essential for civil procedure examinations and court practice.
