Subject: Company Law — Just and Equitable Winding Up, Quasi-Partnership
Source: Ebrahimi v Westbourne Galleries Ltd [1973] AC 360 (House of Lords)

CASE NAME: Ebrahimi v Westbourne Galleries Ltd [1973] AC 360

FACTS:
Ebrahimi and Nazar had run a carpet business in partnership for many years. They later incorporated as a company, Westbourne Galleries Ltd. Each held equal shares and both were directors. Later, Nazar's son joined as a director and shareholder. Ebrahimi and the Nazars fell out. The Nazars used their majority to pass a resolution removing Ebrahimi as a director. He could not participate in management and received no dividend on his shares. He petitioned for winding up on the "just and equitable" ground.

ISSUE:
When will the court order winding up of a company on the just and equitable ground where a member has been excluded from management?

HELD (House of Lords, Lord Wilberforce):
Winding up was ordered. Companies can be based on a personal relationship of mutual trust and confidence — a "quasi-partnership." In such cases, equitable considerations apply beyond the strict legal rights. Exclusion of a member from management in breach of an understanding on which he joined the company may justify winding up on the just and equitable ground.

THE QUASI-PARTNERSHIP DOCTRINE:
A company may be treated as a quasi-partnership where:
1. An association formed on a basis of personal relationship, involving mutual confidence
2. An understanding that all (or some) members would participate in the conduct of the business
3. Restriction on the transfer of shares, so that a member cannot simply exit by selling shares

Where these elements exist, courts will apply equitable considerations beyond strict legal rights — the "just and equitable" ground for winding up gives effect to these.

KEY PRINCIPLES:
1. JUST AND EQUITABLE WINDING UP (Companies and Allied Matters Act, Section 571):
   Court may order winding up where it is just and equitable to do so
2. GROUNDS include:
   (a) Deadlock in management
   (b) Destruction of the substratum (main purpose) of the company
   (c) Justifiable loss of confidence in management
   (d) Exclusion of a member from management in breach of understanding
3. ALTERNATIVE REMEDY: Court may also grant relief under oppression of minority provisions (Section 311 CAMA 2020) — buying out the petitioner's shares instead of winding up

APPLICATION IN NIGERIA:
Ebrahimi v Westbourne Galleries is applied in Nigerian company law in disputes between shareholders of small private companies (quasi-partnerships). Applied under Section 571 CAMA 2020 (just and equitable winding up) and Section 311 CAMA 2020 (unfairly prejudicial conduct). Nigerian courts apply the quasi-partnership doctrine to protect minority shareholders in family companies and small businesses.
