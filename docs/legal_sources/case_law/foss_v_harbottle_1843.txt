Subject: Company Law — Majority Rule and Proper Plaintiff
Source: Foss v Harbottle (1843) 2 Hare 461 — Applied in Nigerian company law

CASE NAME: Foss v Harbottle (1843) 2 Hare 461, 67 ER 189

FACTS:
Two minority shareholders brought an action against directors of Victoria Park Company alleging misapplication of company property and fraud. The company had not authorised the action.

ISSUES:
1. Who is the proper plaintiff when a wrong is done to a company?
2. Can minority shareholders sue for wrongs done to the company?

HELD:
The proper plaintiff for a wrong done to a company is the company itself. Individual shareholders cannot bring an action for a wrong done to the company as a whole. Where the wrong is one the majority could ratify, courts will not interfere at the suit of a minority.

THE TWO RULES IN FOSS v HARBOTTLE:
1. PROPER PLAINTIFF RULE: The company is the proper plaintiff for wrongs done to it
2. MAJORITY RULE: Where an act is capable of confirmation by simple majority, the court will not interfere at the suit of a minority shareholder

EXCEPTIONS (derivative action):
1. Where the act is illegal or ultra vires the company
2. Where the act requires a special majority but was done by a simple majority
3. Where the act constitutes a fraud on the minority and wrongdoers control the company
4. Where the personal rights of individual shareholders are infringed

APPLICATION IN NIGERIA:
Foss v Harbottle is the leading authority on minority shareholder rights in Nigerian company law. Applied under CAMA 2020 Sections 343-355 on minority protection. Nigerian courts apply the rule and its exceptions in shareholder disputes, winding up petitions, and corporate governance litigation.

CITATION: (1843) 2 Hare 461, 67 ER 189
