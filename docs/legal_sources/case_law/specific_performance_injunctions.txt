EQUITABLE REMEDIES IN NIGERIA: SPECIFIC PERFORMANCE AND INJUNCTIONS

EQUITABLE REMEDIES — GENERAL PRINCIPLES:
Equitable remedies are discretionary — unlike the common law right to damages, equity will not grant specific performance or an injunction as of right. The court considers:
1. Whether damages would be an adequate remedy
2. The conduct of the parties (clean hands doctrine)
3. Delay (laches)
4. The balance of hardship

SPECIFIC PERFORMANCE:

DEFINITION:
An order of specific performance compels a party to perform their contractual obligations. It is the primary remedy for breach of a contract for the sale of land.

WHEN SPECIFIC PERFORMANCE IS GRANTED:
1. DAMAGES ARE INADEQUATE: The primary requirement — specific performance is ordered only where monetary compensation would not adequately compensate the claimant
2. LAND AND PROPERTY: Every piece of land is considered unique — specific performance is almost always available for breach of a contract to sell land (Adagun v Faloye — Nigerian authority)
3. UNIQUE GOODS: Specific performance may be ordered for goods with unique characteristics where damages cannot compensate (e.g., a famous painting, a one-of-a-kind item)
4. THE CONTRACT IS VALID AND ENFORCEABLE: The contract must satisfy all requirements — offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality, and any required formalities

WHEN SPECIFIC PERFORMANCE IS REFUSED:
1. CONSTANT SUPERVISION: Contracts requiring ongoing supervision by the court are not specifically enforced (Ryan v Mutual Tontine Association [1893]) — e.g., contracts for personal services
2. PERSONAL SERVICE CONTRACTS: Cannot be specifically enforced (cannot compel a person to work for another) — De Francesco v Barnum (1890)
3. MUTUALITY: Specific performance should be available to both parties — if one party could not have obtained it, it will generally not be granted against the other (subject to exceptions)
4. HARDSHIP: Where enforcement would cause severe hardship to the defendant disproportionate to the benefit to the claimant
5. WANT OF CLEAN HANDS: A claimant who has acted inequitably in connection with the contract will be refused the remedy
6. LACHES: Unreasonable delay by the claimant in seeking the remedy

SPECIFIC PERFORMANCE IN NIGERIA:
- Widely available for breach of contracts for the sale of land — the most common application
- The claimant must show they were ready, willing, and able to perform their own obligations
- Nigerian courts follow English equity principles, applied through the received common law and the Judicature Acts

INJUNCTIONS:

TYPES OF INJUNCTION:

1. PROHIBITORY INJUNCTION:
- Orders the defendant to refrain from doing a specified act
- Most common type — restrains a threatened or continuing wrong
- Examples: restraining breach of contract, nuisance, trespass, defamation

2. MANDATORY INJUNCTION:
- Orders the defendant to do a positive act
- Granted more sparingly than prohibitory injunctions — may require supervision
- Examples: requiring removal of a wall built in breach of covenant

3. INTERLOCUTORY (INTERIM) INJUNCTION:
- Granted before trial to preserve the status quo pending the hearing of the substantive dispute
- Test: American Cyanamid Co v Ethicon Ltd [1975] — is there a serious issue to be tried? Would damages be adequate? What does the balance of convenience require?
- Nigerian test: Kotoye v CBN [1989] — similar principles applied

4. PERPETUAL (FINAL) INJUNCTION:
- Granted at trial as final relief

5. EX PARTE (WITHOUT NOTICE) INJUNCTION:
- Granted urgently without hearing the defendant — only where giving notice would defeat the purpose of the order
- Defendant must be given an early opportunity to apply to discharge

THE CLEAN HANDS DOCTRINE:
"He who comes to equity must come with clean hands."
A claimant who has acted inequitably in connection with the subject matter of the dispute will be refused equitable relief. The misconduct need not be illegal — it must be related to the transaction in question and sufficiently serious.

LACHES:
Delay defeats equity. A claimant who sits on their rights unreasonably and allows the defendant to alter their position will be refused equitable relief. The doctrine does not apply mechanical time limits — it considers whether the delay was unreasonable and prejudicial.

APPLICATION IN NIGERIA:
Specific performance and injunctions are the cornerstone equitable remedies in Nigerian civil litigation. Specific performance is routinely sought in property disputes and commercial contracts. Interlocutory injunctions are a primary tool in commercial, employment, and constitutional law litigation. Nigerian courts have developed a rich jurisprudence on the principles governing these remedies, drawing on English equity while adapting to local conditions.

KEY AUTHORITIES:
- Beswick v Beswick [1968] AC 58 — specific performance available to enforce contract for benefit of a third party
- Lumley v Wagner (1852) 1 De GM & G 604 — injunction to restrain breach of personal service contract (indirect enforcement)
- Kotoye v CBN [1989] — Nigerian principles for interlocutory injunctions
- American Cyanamid v Ethicon [1975] — balance of convenience test
